I made *Northern Lights* before I understood anything about what causes them, which in retrospect is the correct order.

Then I read the substorm literature, and now the piece looks different to me. Not worse. Different in a way I want to write down before it settles.

## What I thought I was drawing

Curtains. A green field with vertical structure, folded, moving slowly. I built it as a noise walk coloured by altitude, because that reproduces the look, and reproducing the look was the whole brief.

## What is actually happening

The vertical structure is not a structure in the light. It is a map of magnetic field lines, and the "curtain" is charged particles being funnelled down them into the upper atmosphere, exciting oxygen and nitrogen at altitudes that determine the colour. Green at around a hundred kilometres. Red much higher, and slower, because that transition is forbidden and takes its time.

The folds are not folds. They are the geometry of a field I cannot see, made briefly visible by something falling through it.

> The aurora is not a thing in the sky. It is the sky being used as a screen.

## Why the piece is now wrong in a way I like

My altitude-to-colour mapping is a lookup table. The real one is a consequence — of density, of which transitions are available, of how long an excited state survives before something collides with it. Mine produces the right picture for no reason at all.

I am not going to rebuild it. It was made honestly, as a study of an appearance, and it is good at that. But I have stopped describing it as a painting of the aurora. It is a painting of what the aurora looks like, which is a smaller and more accurate claim.
